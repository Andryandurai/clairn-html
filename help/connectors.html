Clairn

Linear, Jira Cloud and Google Calendar

Clairn can read your tickets and your calendar to show them next to the project they belong to. Connections are read-only: Clairn never changes anything in Linear, Jira or Google Calendar through them.

Setup note: these connections run through a small sign-in service in the same Supabase project used for device sync. Whoever runs that project has to set it up once. Until then the Connect buttons say that setup is needed. They never pretend to be connected.

What you do

  1. Sign in to the Supabase account used for device sync. Uploading projects is not required.
  2. In Settings choose Linear, Jira or Google Calendar and open the browser sign-in.
  3. Grant read access, return to Clairn, and check that this sign-in succeeded.
  4. Choose the Linear project or team, the Jira project, or the Google calendar, and link it to a Clairn project.
  5. Refresh the link to read the current tickets or schedule. What was read stays available offline, and a failed refresh keeps the last good copy with its time.

What Clairn reads

ServiceWhat you can pickWhat comes back
LinearProjects or teams you can seeIssue ID and title, status, assignee name, whether it is yours, priority, due date, update time, link
Jira CloudProjects in the sites you authoriseIssue key and title, status, assignee name, whether it is yours, priority, due date, update time, link
Google CalendarCalendars on your calendar listEvent title, status, start and end, all-day flag, time zone, link

Descriptions, comments, attachments, attendee lists and files are not requested. Calendar reads cover the last 7 days and the next 30. An upcoming meeting is schedule data, not proof that work happened.

A ticket shown in a project can become a local to-do with Create action. Clairn shows a preview first, reads only the copy saved on your device, and changes nothing at the service.

Privacy

  • The service stores connection details and encrypted sign-in tokens. Tickets and events are handled in memory and sent to your app, not stored by the service.
  • Because the service must use your tokens to read for you, whoever runs it can reach the data those tokens allow. This is separate from the end-to-end encryption used for device sync.
  • Disconnect removes the stored sign-in. For Jira you can also remove the grant in your Atlassian connected-apps settings.

Lists stop at 200 entries and each snapshot at 300 records, and Clairn says so when more exist.

Status: these connections are built and tested with simulated responses. The Linear and Jira connections have not yet been run against real accounts.
